## Port of v1 (phases 0 and 1)

- `src/app.ts` is v1's script moved over nearly verbatim (with `@ts-nocheck`) instead of a typed rewrite.
  Reason: the brief says keep v1's content, logic and cost math; a rewrite risks changing them.
  `data/models.json` was checked to be identical to the data embedded in v1.
- v1's SVG/canvas engines are kept in `src/legacy-engines.ts` as the fallback when a world fails to load
  (for example no WebGL). v1's SVG effort diagrams stay in the page under the live scene for the same
  reason and as the accessible description.
- One engine per world draws both the scroll story and that world's effort scenes. Reason: only one
  WebGL context alive at a time, and effort scenes reuse the same models and shaders.
- ScrollTrigger scrubs the scroll position and Lenis smooths scrolling; the worlds interpolate between
  keyframes themselves rather than each owning a GSAP timeline. Reason: one clock, simpler disposal.
- Reduced motion: no Lenis, no scrub smoothing, worlds are drawn at time 0 as static plates.

## Let Effort choose (chapter 6)

  it at build time, so a deployed copy is a snapshot of the rules at build.
- Effort's ceiling is High and it only knows coding words; the chapter says both plainly.
- The guide's model rule (in `src/choose.ts`): short mechanical task -> lightest model; high-stakes AND
  tangled -> most capable available model; otherwise the family's "Start here" model. Reason: mirrors the
  guide's own advice in chapters 1 and 4 and does not depend on anything Effort sells.
- The suggested model comes from the family selected in the header. Reason: the guide never ranks one
  company over another.
- The prompt box is capped at 2,000 characters and nothing typed is stored or sent.

## Worlds

- Workers: the OpenAI, Gemini and Claude worlds were written by Codex (GPT-6.1 Sol, medium) from briefs,
  then checked in a real browser and sent back for fixes. Art was generated by Codex's image tool
  (GPT-6 Luna session). Gemini/Antigravity was not used for images: its headless CLI exposes no image
  tool I could verify, and Codex's output was already consistent in style.
- Earth day map is 2K only (NASA SVS 3615). Reason: NASA's eoimages server, which hosts the 5400px Blue
  Marble files, was unreachable today. Night lights and Moon have 2K and 4K.
- Earth clouds are derived from two NASA frames (with and without clouds) because the standard Blue
  Marble cloud map is on the unreachable server. The ocean glint mask is computed in the shader.
- Moon relief uses NASA's 8-bit elevation JPEG as a bump map (the float TIFF could not be converted here).
- Cut-outs use a flood fill from the image border instead of rembg. Reason: the art was generated on a
  flat paper background, the result is clean, and rembg's install plus its 170 MB model stalled on this
  network. `scripts/process-art.py --rembg` still supports it.
- Butterflies are one image each, split down the middle and flapped as two halves, instead of separate
  forewing, hindwing and body layers. Reason: separately generated parts would not match each other.
- Pebbles, lily pads and small butterflies were generated as rows and split, to keep them consistent.
- There is no paper image; the sheet of paper is drawn in code.
- Solar prominences are stylized loops, not a fluid simulation. Reason: phone performance.

## Design-review fixes (16 Nov 2026)
